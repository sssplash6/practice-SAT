# Rating — duels

Decided 2026-09-20. The ladder is fixed; the badge artwork is not (see below).

## The ladder

| level | rating |
|---:|---|
| 1 | 1 – 800 |
| 2 | 801 – 950 |
| 3 | 951 – 1100 |
| 4 | 1101 – 1250 |
| 5 | 1251 – 1400 |
| 6 | 1401 – 1550 |
| 7 | 1551 – 1700 |
| 8 | 1701 – 1850 |
| 9 | 1851 – 2000 |
| 10 | 2001 + |

Bands are 150 wide from level 2 to level 9. Level 1 is the floor (everything
below 801) and level 10 is open at the top, so the ladder has a catch-all at
each end and eight equal steps between.

## Where a new player starts

**Seed rating 1000, which is level 3.** The first three duels are placement:
they move the rating more than a normal duel does, and the level shown is
provisional until they are done.

Starting at level 3 rather than level 1 is deliberate and worth keeping. A
player who begins at the bottom can only go up, which makes early losses free.
Starting two levels off the floor means a rating can fall from the first duel —
so the number is real from day one, and the floor is a demotion rather than a
starting line.

## Per duel

* Each duel moves rating up or down by performance (standard ELO expectation
  against the opponent's rating; K larger during placement, smaller after).
* Five questions per duel, universal time.
* Rooms are private or public; public rooms can be spectated.
* Matching is system-based on rating.
* Win rate, streak and similar are tracked but are decoration, not rating.

## The badge — a decision still open

The reference ladder was illustrated with a third party's level badges (the
gauge-and-digit dials are a well-known esports platform's). **The structure is
ours to adopt; the artwork is not.** Ten levels, a fill that rises with level,
and the digit inside are all fine to keep. The drawing must be our own.

Proposed, not built: **the brand mark is the badge.** The mark is a ring with a
large central counter and four gaps — the counter takes the level digit, and
the ring fills in ten steps from nearly empty at level 1 to complete at level
10. Brand orange on a graphite track throughout, no rainbow: progression is
shown by *how much* is filled, not by a colour ramp, which keeps the orange
meaning one thing across the product and keeps a level-8 badge from looking
like a primary button. Level 10 may invert to a solid orange square, as a
ceiling should look different from a step.

This also gives the duel front page its element: the badge in the
**uncalibrated state** — ring empty, counter blank — with the button reading
"Calibrate". After three duels the same page shows the player's real level.
