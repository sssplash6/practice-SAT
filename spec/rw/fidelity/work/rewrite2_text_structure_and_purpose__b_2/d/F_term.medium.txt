<u>Many trees reproduce by mast seeding, or masting: individuals across a population synchronously produce enormous seed crops in some years and very few in others.</u> Bumper years satiate seed predators, allowing many seeds to escape consumption. Analyzing nearly four decades of seed production by European beech trees in England, ecologist Michał Bogdziewicz and colleagues found that as the climate warmed, the trees produced more seed overall but varied less from year to year and less in unison, while losses to seed-eating moth larvae climbed. The principal beneficiaries of warming, the researchers conclude, are not the trees but their predators.
