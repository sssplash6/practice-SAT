The following text is from Ivan Turgenev’s 1859 novel A House of Gentlefolk (translated by Constance Garnett in 1894). Lavretsky has returned to his home province in Russia after separating from his unfaithful wife.

Lavretsky certainly did not look like the victim of fate. His rosy-cheeked typical Russian face, with its large white brow, rather thick nose, and wide straight lips seemed breathing with the wild health of the steppes, with vigorous primaeval energy. He was splendidly well-built, and his fair curly hair stood up on his head like a boy’s. <u>It was only in his blue eyes, with their overhanging brows and somewhat fixed look, that one could trace an expression, not exactly of melancholy, nor exactly of weariness</u>, and his voice had almost too measured a cadence.
