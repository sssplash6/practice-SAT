Rock varnish is a dark coating that forms on exposed rock surfaces in deserts worldwide. <u>Its most perplexing feature is its composition: varnish typically contains two to three orders of magnitude more manganese than the underlying rock or the surrounding dust from which much of its mass derives.</u> Analyzing varnish from the western United States, geobiologist Usha Lingappa and colleagues found it rich in cyanobacteria of the genus Chroococcidiopsis. These microbes accumulate manganese in extraordinary amounts, apparently using it as an antioxidant. Such uptake by the coating’s earliest inhabitants, the researchers propose, accounts for its enrichment.
