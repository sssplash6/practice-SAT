In 2016, while her brother, the reindeer herder Jovsset Ánte Sara, was contesting a state-mandated cull that he argued would render his livelihood economically unviable, Sámi artist Máret Ánne Sara heaped 200 reindeer heads from a slaughterhouse outside the courthouse in Tana Bru, Norway, and planted a Norwegian flag on top. That intervention inaugurated Pile o’Sápmi, an ongoing project whose subsequent works include a flag fashioned from hundreds of bullet-pierced reindeer skulls. Exhibited alongside an 1892 photograph of a mound of bison skulls, the project positions the cull within a longer history of colonial dispossession.
