The following text is adapted from Jessie Redmon Fauset’s 1924 novel There Is Confusion. Joanna Marshall, a dancer, has become a star on Broadway.

[Joanna] was beginning to feel the need of something with which to fill her life. But life, she began to realize, was not a matter of sufficient raiment, food, or even success. There must be something more filling, more insistent, more permeating—the sort of thing that left no room for boredom or introspection. For in spite of her vogue, her unbelievably decided successes, Joanna frequently tasted the depths of ennui. She saw life as a ghastly skeleton and herself feverishly trying to cover up its bare bones with the garish trappings of her art, her lessons, her practice, her press-clippings.
