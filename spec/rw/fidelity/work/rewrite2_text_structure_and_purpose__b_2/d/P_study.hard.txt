Because a bird mother can no longer alter her embryos’ biochemical environment once her eggs are laid, it has been unclear whether avian parents can prepare their offspring for prevailing climatic conditions. Ecologists Mylene Mariette and Katherine Buchanan found that incubating zebra finches emit a distinctive call to their eggs late in incubation, and only when temperatures exceed 26°C. Embryos exposed to recordings of this call grew less in hotter nests as nestlings, a pattern the researchers linked to greater reproductive success, and as adults they consistently bred in hotter nest boxes than birds that had heard a different call.
