Metamaterials, structures whose unusual physical properties arise from the arrangement of many tiny elements rather than from the materials they are made of, are rare in nature, and until recently no acoustic example was known. Biologist Thomas Neil and colleagues have found that the scale layer on moths’ wings is one: <u>each scale acts as a resonator tuned to a specific frequency, and because the scales are coupled through the wing membrane, together they absorb ultrasound across the full range of frequencies that bats use in echolocation</u>. This acoustic camouflage is achieved with a layer thin and light enough for flight.
