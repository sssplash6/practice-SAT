Although some clay tablets inscribed by scribes in ancient Mesopotamia were deliberately fired in kilns, rendering them hard and durable, many were merely dried in the open air, which left them fragile but also allowed them to be soaked in water and reshaped for reuse. Much of the library that the Assyrian king Ashurbanipal assembled at Nineveh in the seventh century BCE was housed in palaces that burned when a coalition of Babylonians, Medes, and Scythians sacked the city in 612 BCE. Excavators working at the site from 1849 onward recovered more than 30,000 tablets and fragments.
